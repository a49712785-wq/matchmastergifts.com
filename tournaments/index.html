Match Masters Tournaments (2026): How to Win, Prizes & Best Strategy

Last updated: November 18, 2026

Quick answer: Tournaments are Match Masters' knockout mode: 8 to 64 players enter, you play head-to-head matches in blocks of 4, and one loss can end your run. How tournaments work

A tournament is a knockout bracket, not a normal match. You join, the game fills the player spots (8 to 64 players depending on the tournament), and the bracket starts as soon as it is full. Matches are played in blocks of 4: you face one opponent, and if you win, your next opponent is the winner of the other match in your block. Keep winning and you keep advancing; lose once and your tournament is over — there is no second chance in the same bracket.

That structure changes everything about how you should play. In a normal match, a loss costs you some trophies and you queue again. In a tournament, a loss costs you the entry and the prizes you were chasing. Every move matters more, and the booster you locked in at the start has to carry you through every round, on boards you cannot predict.

Tournaments unlock at 1,500 trophies. Before that, the mode simply is not there — focus on normal matches and the Trophy Road until you reach it. Once unlocked, tournaments appear in the Events tab, each with its own banner card showing the player count, the rules, and the prizes.

How to enter (step by step)

Before you tap Join

  1. Open the Events tab and find the tournament card. Do not tap Join yet.
  2. Tap the "i" icon. Read the rules: player count, modifiers, whether it is ranked or non-ranked, and any special rule for this tournament.
  3. Tap the "Prizes" icon. Read the full prize list — what the winner gets, what runners-up get, and whether there are prizes for reaching a round even if you do not win it all.
  4. Check the entry cost on the card. It is paid in coins and it varies by tournament — never assume it is the same as the last one.
  5. Pick your booster and perk BEFORE you join. This is the decision that decides your tournament. Once you are in, you cannot change either one.
  6. Join only when you have time to play every round. A tournament that starts while you are busy is an entry fee thrown away.
  7. Play your first match as soon as the bracket fills. Your opponent is waiting, and the round will not wait for you.

What you can win

Tournament prizes vary — that is why the Prizes icon exists — but the prize types are consistent: Event Points, Tournament Cups, boosters, and trophies. Some tournaments also pay coins and sticker packs along the way. Two things to know:

Ranked tournaments pay stars and trophies per match, just like normal matches. Non-ranked tournaments do not pay trophies per match — check the card for the non-ranked icon — but the tournament's own prizes can still include trophies.

The biggest prizes sit at the end, but many tournaments pay something for reaching the later rounds. A tournament where you win two rounds and lose the third can still be worth more than the entry — read the prize list before you decide it is winner-takes-all.

Entry cost — what to check first

There is no single entry fee for Match Masters tournaments. Each tournament sets its own cost in coins, and the cost is shown on its banner card. The honest rule is simple: if the entry costs more coins than you can earn back from a normal evening of matches, only enter if the prize list is worth it to you. Coins are the same currency you spend on boosters and sticker packs — our free-coins guide covers every free way to refill them — so a tournament entry is a real trade-off, not a free roll. Never enter a tournament on an empty coin balance and hope the prizes refill you; they only pay if you win rounds.

Picking your booster and perk

This is the whole tournament, decided before it starts. Three rules:

1

Pick a booster you trust on ANY board

You do not know what the boards will look like, what modifiers will be active, or what your opponent will bring. A booster that is brilliant on one board type and useless on another is a bad tournament pick. A reliable all-rounder — one you have actually won with before — beats a flashy one you have only tried once.

2

Pick a perk that helps from the first move

Perks fire early and set the tone of the match. A perk that only helps in a specific board state may never get its moment in a short knockout match.

3

Do not experiment in a tournament

New booster, new perk, new strategy — test all of that in normal matches, where a loss is cheap. Tournaments are where you play what you already know works.

If you are unsure which booster is your reliable one, look at your recent wins, not your collection. The booster you win the most normal matches with is your tournament booster. Our best boosters ranked page shows which boosters the community rates highest — and which ones are best by game mode.

Modifiers and special rules

Many tournaments run with modifiers — extra rules layered on top of the normal match. A match can have up to three modifiers at once, and they can change which booster is strong. The tournament card lists them, and the "i" icon explains them. Read them every time, even if you have played the same tournament before — the modifiers can be different each time. A booster that charges slowly is a liability under a modifier that shortens the match; a board-clearing booster is gold under a modifier that floods the board with one colour. The players who win tournaments are usually the ones who read the modifiers and picked for them, not the ones with the rarest booster.

Strategy: how to win more

1

Win the first match calmly

The first round is against a random player from your block, and nerves lose more first rounds than skill does. Play your normal game — do not burn your booster in the first three moves unless the board demands it.

2

Charge, then strike

Your booster charges as you match its colour. In a knockout match, the player who fires their booster at the right moment — when the board is set up for it, not the moment it lights up — usually wins the round.

3

Watch your opponent's colour, not just yours

Every match you make in your opponent's colour charges their booster. If you can win the round without feeding their colour, do it.

4

Save your best booster for the tournament, not the warm-up

If you only have one copy of your best booster, do not spend it on a normal match ten minutes before you enter.

5

Play rounds back-to-back

Momentum is real in a knockout bracket: you are warm, you have seen the modifiers, and your head is in the game. A long break between rounds is how a winning run cools off.

Mistakes that end your run

1

Entering with an untested booster

Tournaments lock your loadout. A booster you have never won with is a gamble, and the entry fee is the stake.

2

Not reading the Prizes icon

Some tournaments pay well for reaching the semi-final; others pay almost nothing unless you win it all. The same entry fee can be a good deal or a bad one depending on that list.

3

Entering when you cannot finish

A tournament starts when the bracket fills, and rounds follow quickly. Joining before a meeting, a commute, or bedtime is how entries get wasted.

4

Ignoring the modifiers

The card tells you the rules. Players who skip that step pick a booster for a normal match and lose to a rule they never read.

5

Chasing losses

One tournament loss is not a reason to immediately re-enter another. The entry fee is gone either way — take a breath, check your coins, and decide with a clear head.

6

Spending your last coins on entry

If the entry leaves you unable to buy the booster you need for normal matches, the tournament has cost you more than its fee.

When NOT to enter

You are below 1,500 trophies (the mode is locked anyway — keep climbing first). You cannot read the prize list and say, out loud, what you get for reaching the round you usually reach — if you cannot, you have not read it. Your coin balance cannot absorb losing the entry fee — tournaments are a gamble even for good players, and a bad board can end any run. You have ten minutes, not an hour — knockout rounds do not pause for your schedule. You are tilted from a loss — tournament play punishes rushed, emotional moves more than any other mode.

Tournaments FAQ

How do I unlock tournaments in Match Masters?

Reach 1,500 trophies. Tournaments then appear in the Events tab. Before that, keep climbing the Trophy Road with normal matches.

How many players are in a Match Masters tournament?

Between 8 and 64, depending on the tournament. The exact number is on the tournament's banner card.

How does a Match Masters tournament work?

A knockout bracket in blocks of 4. Win your match and you face the winner of the other match in your block; lose once and your run ends. Your booster and perks are locked for the whole tournament.

Can I change my booster during a tournament?

No. Once you join, your booster and perk selections cannot be changed, so pick them carefully before you tap Join.

What do you win in a Match Masters tournament?

Event Points, Tournament Cups, boosters and trophies, with exact prizes varying per tournament. Tap the Prizes icon on the banner to see the full list before entering.

Do tournament matches give trophies?

Normally yes — ranked tournament matches pay stars and trophies like normal matches. Non-ranked tournaments do not pay trophies per match (check for the non-ranked icon), though their own prizes can still include trophies.

How much does it cost to enter a tournament?

It varies by tournament and is paid in coins. There is no fixed fee — the cost is shown on the tournament card, so read it before joining and never enter with your last coins.
