Match Masters is free to download, and that is the first thing every new player learns — and the second thing they learn is that coins run out. You equip a Diamond booster for a big match, you pay a tournament entry fee, you buy an extra Lucky Spin, and suddenly the coin balance that looked comfortable yesterday is gone. At that exact moment the shop is right there, one tap away, selling you more coins. So the question every player eventually asks is the honest one: is buying coins actually worth it, or does free play cover everything?

This guide answers that with numbers where numbers exist, and honesty where they don't. Every price in it comes from a real listing. Every coin cost comes from our own cost table. And where no public source states a figure — like how many coins are inside a coin pack — we say so, instead of inventing one.

Read that table as a budget, not a price list. The value test: three questions

Forget generic advice. Run your situation through these three questions and the answer falls out by itself.

1

Do you need the coins now, or can you wait a day?

If the answer is "I want to play a Diamond match right now," buying is the only way to get coins instantly — free income is capped per day and cannot be rushed. If the answer is "I just want a comfortable balance for the week," waiting wins: a week of daily links, spins, and wins is free, and the coins arrive whether you pay or not. Most "I need coins" moments are impatience, not shortage. Sleep on it once, and half of them disappear.

2

What are you buying the coins for?

Coins spent on a Diamond booster for a match you care about are the best spend in the game — the booster directly improves your odds. Coins spent on tournament entry are the second-best, because a good placement returns multiples of the fee. Coins spent on extra Lucky Spins are the worst spend: the wheel is random, the payout is usually small, and you are paying coins for a chance at coins. If your purchase is funding spins, stop and rethink.

3

Are you buying coins, or a bundle?

The shop sells raw coin packs and it sells offers that bundle coins with boosters, lives, and spins. Before you buy a raw coin pack, look at what the bundle at the same price includes. A bundle that contains boosters you would have bought anyway is usually the better spend than the same money in raw coins — you get the coins and the boosters for one price. That is a rule of thumb, not a measured fact, because the bundle contents rotate with events and offers. But the habit — always compare the bundle first — costs nothing and saves real money over time.

When buying makes sense

Buying is the right call in a narrow set of situations, and they are all the same shape: you play a lot, at a high level, and time matters more than money to you.

✅

You play Diamond-level matches every day. Your burn rate is 300–1,000 coins per Diamond purchase, and free income cannot sustain that. A small, regular purchase — the $1.99 Stack, not the $9.99 Case — closes the gap without overspending.

✅

You are chasing a limited-time event or tournament. Events end. Albums close. A tournament with a prize pool that pays multiples of the entry fee is the one place where spending coins can be an investment rather than consumption, and if you are short of the entry fee on the last day, buying in is rational.

✅

You have a hard budget and you stick to it. Buying works when it is a planned, capped expense — the same as any hobby budget. It fails when it is an impulse tap after a lost match.

When buying does not make sense

1

You play casually, a few matches a day

Bronze is free, Silver and Gold are affordable on free earnings, and your coin balance grows on its own. Buying would be paying for coins you were going to get anyway.

2

You are buying to recover from a loss

This is the most expensive habit in the game. A lost match does not make the next match more winnable, and coins spent angry are coins spent badly. Take a break; the free spin and the daily links will still be there tomorrow.

3

You have not claimed today's free gifts yet

Check the daily gift links first — every single day — before opening the shop. Buying coins while unclaimed free coins are sitting on the table is the one mistake with no excuse.

4

You are buying the smallest pack over and over

Check the ladder before you repeat-buy: the three named coin packs are $1.99, $4.99, and $9.99, and the bigger packs exist for a reason. If you are going to spend anyway, one larger purchase usually beats five small ones — but verify the coin amount on the buy screen first, because the store is the only place that number exists.

If you buy: five rules

The five rules

  1. Set the budget before you open the shop. Decide the weekly amount in advance. When it is gone, it is gone until next week.
  2. Compare the bundle first. Raw coins first, bundles second is the wrong order. Look at the offers at your price point before touching a coin pack.
  3. Never buy from outside the game. Only the in-game store sells coins. Third-party coin sellers, "cheap coin" sites, and anyone offering coins for cash outside the game are scams — they take your money, and they can get your account banned. Candivore sells coins in exactly one place: the shop inside Match Masters.
  4. Check the buy screen, not a guide. Prices rotate with seasons, events, and balance updates, and they vary by region. The price and the coin amount on the button are the truth. If a guide's numbers disagree with the button, the button is right.
  5. Watch the spend, not the balance. A healthy coin balance feels good and means nothing. What matters is the weekly outflow: if you are spending more coins per week than free play plus purchases bring in, the balance is falling no matter how big it looks today.

If you don't buy: the free routine that works

Free play is not a consolation prize — for most players it is the whole game, and it works if you treat it as a routine instead of a hope. Claim the daily gift links first thing (they expire within a couple of days, and they are the single biggest free source). Take the free Lucky Spin every day. Play your matches with the booster tier your balance supports, not the tier you wish it supported — Bronze is free, and a won Bronze match pays more than a lost Diamond one. Finish album pages, because the completion bonuses pay coins on top of booster bundles. And join a team: teammates trade duplicate stickers, and traded duplicates become completed pages, which become coins.

That routine does not make you rich. It makes you solvent — and solvent players beat broke players, because a broke player cannot equip the booster the match needs, while a solvent player always can.

The scams to avoid

Two scams target exactly the player this article is for — the player who wants coins and is deciding whether to pay. The first is the "unlimited coin generator": a site or video that promises free coins if you enter your username, complete an offer, or download an app. Every single one is a scam. They harvest logins or install malware, and no generator has ever delivered a coin. The second is the third-party coin seller: a site that sells Match Masters coins cheaper than the in-game shop. The coins do not exist, the seller takes your payment, and if anything did arrive it would be against the game's rules. Real free coins only come from the game itself — gift links, spins, wins, albums, events — and real paid coins only come from the shop inside the game. Everything else is someone else's payday.

The verdict

So: is buying coins in Match Masters worth it? For most players, no — not because buying is a rip-off, but because free play already covers the game they are actually playing. Bronze is free, Silver and Gold are affordable on daily earnings, and a five-minute daily routine of links, spins, and wins keeps the balance healthy. Buying only starts to make sense when your play outgrows free income: Diamond boosters every match, tournaments every day, events you cannot afford to miss. And even then, the right purchase is small, planned, and compared against the bundle at the same price — never an angry tap after a loss, never a raw coin pack bought before checking the offers, and never from anywhere outside the game.

The game is free. The coins are the bottleneck by design. Whether you pay to widen it or wait for the daily refill is a budget decision, not a skill decision — and now you have the numbers to make it honestly.
